This is the teardown for [the system I built in June](/archive/diy-drip-irrigation-raised-beds/): seven raised beds on one zone of a B-hyve XD hose timer, about 140 feet of emitter line. The check valves that kept that line full all summer are the reason it has to come out. They hold water in it all winter too, and water that freezes in a fitting splits it.

## What broke

Two things snagged me all season, and both of them are the timer.

**The scroll wheel flips to hours.** The XD sets run time with a scroll wheel. Roll it past 59 minutes and it sometimes stops counting minutes and starts counting hours. On August 18 the app logged Zone 4, the raised beds, at nearly 900 minutes. That is fifteen hours, and the likeliest explanation is that wheel. **[ALEX: did the beds show it, or did you catch it? One line.]** Read the display before you walk away from it.

**Some runs quit after about 30 seconds.** I found it in the app's watering log: two days, **[DATES]**, where a Zone 4 run started from the app over WiFi shut off after 20 to 40 seconds. The log recorded them as short runs, so at least the log was honest.

On teardown day I tried to make it happen again. I started Zone 4 from the wheel at the manifold, from the app over Bluetooth, and from the app over WiFi, and let each run about a minute. All three ran clean.

My first guess was the network. But my own Bluetooth script, which never touches the WiFi, read one zone as idle about 30 seconds into a 60-second test on September 8. That was a status reading, not water I watched stop, so it proves little, but it makes WiFi a weaker suspect. **[VERIFY: that the September 8 early idle was zone 4. hestia's deploy/BHYVE.md says "one zone" without naming it.]**

The plan from the build was to let the soil sensors drive Zone 4 on their own. A fault that showed up twice in a season and never on demand is the worst kind for that, because no test can clear it. The sensors can catch it, though: a real run moves the moisture reading, and a 30-second one doesn't.
