On **September 4, 2026**, Google Assistant goes away on phones, tablets, Wear OS watches, wireless headphones, and Android Auto projected from a phone. Gemini replaces it. Google says the rollout may take a few weeks to reach everyone.

Your Nest Mini is not on that list. Neither is your Home Mini, your Nest Audio, your Nest Hub, or your ten-year-old original Google Home. Speakers and displays are on a separate track, and no hardware is being bricked.

The loudest version of this story going around right now is wrong, and the true version is worse.

## What actually stops working on September 4

- **Android phones and tablets.** Gemini becomes the assistant.
- **Wear OS watches.**
- **Wireless headphones** paired to a phone.
- **Android Auto**, when it is projected from your phone.

One exception worth knowing: **cars with Google built-in keep Assistant** past September 4. If the software lives in the dashboard rather than in your pocket, you are not affected, yet.

Once it is gone from your phone, Google is explicit that you cannot use it or switch back.

## What does not stop working

Smart speakers and displays are moving to Gemini for Home, which is a different rollout on a different clock. It is running as an early access program across 19 countries and 8 languages, and it is still expanding.

The support list is genuinely broad. Full Gemini for Home runs on the Google Home Speaker (2026), Nest Hub (1st and 2nd gen), Nest Hub Max, Nest Audio, Nest Mini (2nd gen), and the **Google Home Mini (1st gen)**, a speaker from 2017. Older kit including the original Google Home, Home Max, Nest Wifi point and third-party speakers gets most features, minus Gemini Live.

Read that list again if you came here from a thread about e-waste.

The grievance underneath is real, it just is not e-waste. The speaker on your counter cannot be pointed at anything else, locked bootloader and no local API worth the name, so it keeps working perfectly, for Google.

## The part that should actually bother you

Gemini for Home starts as opt-in. You get an invitation, you accept it. So far so reasonable.

Here is the sentence from Google's own help page:

> Once you switch your home to Gemini for Home voice assistant, it can't switch back to Google Assistant.

And alongside it, that the switch applies to the whole household at once: once one person flips it, "it'll be immediately available to everyone in your home."

So the choice is offered exactly once, it is made for everyone under your roof, and it is permanent. Not permanent as in hard to undo. Permanent as in there is no control anywhere that undoes it.

Whether you like Gemini is beside the point. You might prefer it. Plenty of people do. The structural fact is that a piece of software you depend on daily can be replaced with different software, on hardware you paid for and own outright, on a schedule you do not set, with no rollback. That is true whether the replacement is better or worse, and it will be true again the next time.

This is the thing homesteaders already understand about seed. If the only place a thing can come from is a catalog, you do not have a supply, you have a subscription. [The same argument, applied to knowledge](/archive/data-sovereignty-is-seed-saving/), holds here for software.

## About putting the device in airplane mode

Some people are cutting their speakers off the network to block the OTA update. It does delay the change, and I understand the instinct.

Be clear about the trade though. A Google speaker with no internet is a Bluetooth speaker with a good microphone it cannot use. You keep Assistant by disabling nearly everything Assistant does. That buys time, and it is worth doing if you need weeks to plan a move, but it is not a place to live.

The honest framing is that it is a stall, not a fix.

## The actual exit, briefly

If the one-way door is what bothers you, the fix is not a different vendor with a different door. It is running the assistant on hardware you control, where the wake word, the speech-to-text, the logic and the voice all stay in your house.

That is a real option now, and it is not the soldering project it was three years ago. I have been running exactly this setup as my kitchen speaker since June, wake word to transcription to answer to speech, nothing leaving the building, and I will write it up with the parts list, the costs and the things that annoy me about it.

What I would do before September 4:

1. **Know which of your devices are actually affected.** Phone yes, speaker not yet.
2. **Do not accept the Gemini for Home invitation reflexively.** It is the only time you get asked, and it decides for your whole household.
3. **If you were going to try local voice anyway, start while you still have a working fallback.** Migrating in a panic is how people end up with no assistant and a bad weekend.

None of this is an emergency. It is a deadline, which is a better thing, because deadlines can be planned for.
